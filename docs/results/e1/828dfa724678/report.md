# E1: Recall and ranking baselines on Amazon Reviews 2023 Video_Games

Commit `828dfa724678` · generated by `make eval-e1` · 2026-11-26T04:48:43Z

Data: Hou et al., *Bridging Language and Items for Retrieval and Recommendation*, arXiv:2403.03952, 2024 (see `docs/data-card.md`). Aggregates only; no review text.

## Question

Which recall / ranking approach works on a leave-last-out temporal split? Hypothesis (SPEC §8.1): hybrid > popularity.

## Answer

- On the test set (94,762 users, full ranking over 25,612 items), `hybrid_rrf` **beats** the popularity baseline on NDCG@10, and the full pipeline (`hybrid_rrf_ranker_mmr`) **beats** it (paired bootstrap 95% CI).
- Best arm by NDCG@10: `hybrid_rrf_ranker` (0.0533 [0.0522, 0.0546]).
- Rating >= 4 robustness variant (74,022 users): `hybrid_rrf` beats popularity; full pipeline beats popularity.

## Method

- **Split** (P1, data card §6): per user, last interaction = test, second-to-last = validation, rest = train. Every review counts as a positive interaction.
- **Tuning** on a seeded subsample of 20,000 validation users (history = train). Grids are in `results.json`. Choices are frozen before the test run.
- **Test, once:** models refit on train + valid; history = train + valid; one held-out item per user; all users.
- **Full ranking:** each list is ranked over the whole catalog minus the user's seen items; no sampled negatives (ADR-0009). Ties: lower item_id first.
- **Availability filter is off in evaluation**: the stock is simulated (FR-D3), and removing out-of-stock items would delete real ground truth. The filter is unit tested (FR-R2) and on by default in the serving pipeline.
- **Statistics:** percentile bootstrap over users, 1000 resamples, seed 20261125; differences vs popularity are paired. Recall@K equals HitRate@K here because each user has exactly one target. Coverage is a catalog-level number and has no CI.
- **Arms:** `popularity` (train counts); `covisit` (item-item co-occurrence within a window of each user's sequence); `embedding` (content vectors from title, features, details and description; never reviews); `hybrid_rrf` (weighted RRF of the three); `hybrid_rrf_ranker` (explainable feature-weighted ranker over the top 100 RRF candidates); `hybrid_rrf_ranker_mmr` (plus MMR diversity re-ranking).
- **Embedding model:** `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5@5c38ec7c405ec4b44b94cc5a9bb96e735b38267a:seq256` on CPU; vectors cached on disk (sha256 `e984cb44d91754b4…`).

## Limitations

- **Global-timeline caveat.** Leave-last-out splits per user, not at one global cut-off date. For the median test event, 19.4% of training interactions happen later in calendar time (100.0% of test events have at least one later training interaction). Popularity and co-visitation therefore see some "future" trends; absolute numbers are optimistic compared with a global temporal split, and popularity benefits most.
- **Rating >= 4 variant.** The main result counts 1-3 star reviews as positive signals (owner decision, data card §10). The variant shows how the ranking of arms changes when they are excluded; it is not tuned separately, and its user set is smaller and different, so its numbers are not directly comparable with the main table.
- **Reviews are a proxy for purchases**; 5-core filtering keeps active users and popular items. Offline metrics only; no claim about online CTR/CVR (SPEC §14).
- **One held-out item per user** makes per-user metrics binary and noisy; the CIs reflect that. Coverage has no CI.
- **Small tuning grids** on a 20k-user validation subsample; a larger search could move every learned arm. Popularity has nothing to tune, so the comparison slightly favors the tuned arms on validation, but test numbers are untouched by tuning.
- **Simulated inventory** is not used in evaluation (see Method).
- **Embedding text** truncated to 256 tokens (title, bullets and details come first); description tails are not embedded.

## What was tried and did not work

- **Cosine-normalized co-visitation.** Expected to help by damping popular items; it was
  worse in every validation cell (NDCG@10 0.034-0.043 vs 0.045-0.053 for raw counts).
  On this 5-core data, popular neighbors are usually the right answer. Raw counts are kept.
- **Popularity as an RRF route.** Every non-zero popularity weight lowered validation
  NDCG@10 (best with weight 0: 0.0543; 0.25: 0.0532; 0.5: 0.0505). The tuned hybrid
  fuses co-visitation and content only. Popularity still enters as a weak ranker feature
  (weight 0.25).
- **Heavier content weight in RRF.** Embedding weights 1.0 and 2.0 were worse than 0.5.
  On its own the content route is weak: NDCG@10 0.0167 vs 0.0127 for popularity on test,
  and in the rating >= 4 variant its Recall@10 gain over popularity is not significant.
- **Long content profiles.** A mean over the last 20 items without decay was the worst
  embedding setting (0.0121). Strong recency (decay 0.5) helps, and then 5 vs 20 items
  makes no difference: users' next item resembles their last one or two, not their
  history.
- **MMR diversity.** The gain is small. On validation, lambda 0.5 raises ILD@10 from 0.309
  to 0.334 for about 0.5% NDCG@10. On test, the MMR arm is slightly below the
  non-diversified ranker on NDCG@10 (0.0529 vs 0.0533; the intervals overlap) and
  recovers about half of the category diversity the ranker loses (0.415 -> 0.465, vs
  0.500 for plain RRF).
- **Reason tags are skewed by weights.** With the tuned content weight of 2.0,
  `similar_content` is 71% of all tags, while `co_visited_with_history` is under 1%, even
  though co-visitation drives most of the candidates. Tags explain the *score
  decomposition*; they are not causal evidence. Normalizing contributions per feature
  before tagging is a possible fix and was not tried.
- **Environment.** The newest torch (2.12+) has no macOS 13 wheel. torch is capped
  at < 2.12, which brings three accepted audit findings (ADR-0012).
